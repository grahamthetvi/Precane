How to Build Your Precane

Building a PVC precane is a simple DIY project that requires basic tools. Here is a general guide to assembling your precane using the dimensions calculated above.

Materials Needed:

  • Schedule 40 PVC Pipe (Use the diameter recommended in your results, usually 1/2" or 3/4")
  • 2 PVC 90-degree elbows (for the handle turns)
  • PVC Cement (optional, for permanent assembly)
  • PVC Cutter or Hacksaw
  • Measuring Tape and Marker
  • Sandpaper (to smooth rough edges)

Instructions:

1. Measure and Cut: Using the calculated dimensions, cut your PVC pipe into three main pieces: the handle (Leg A), the preview extension (Leg B), and the main shaft (Leg C). Take care to account for the depth of the elbow joints (typically about 0.5 to 1 inch of pipe goes inside the fitting) so your final lengths remain accurate.

2. Dry Fit the Joints: Before gluing anything, assemble the cane to ensure it feels right. Connect the shaft (C) to the preview extension (B) using a 90-degree elbow. Then, connect the preview extension (B) to the handle (A) using the second 90-degree elbow. The result should resemble an open hook or offset cane shape.

3. Test and Adjust: Have the user try the cane. The handle should rest comfortably near the hip or waist (depending on preference), and the tip of the shaft should rest on the ground ahead of them. If it feels too long or short, adjust the cuts now.

4. Final Assembly: Once satisfied with the fit, you can use PVC cement to permanently glue the joints. If you prefer to keep the cane adjustable or easy to disassemble for travel, leave it friction-fit (unglued) and ensure the joints are pressed together tightly.

Note: For a more durable tip, you can cover the bottom end of the main shaft with a PVC cap, or use a rubber cane tip that fits snugly over the pipe.
